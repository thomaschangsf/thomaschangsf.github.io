# My Writing Style

Observations from the blog posts "Making Better Predictions" and "Thinking About Thinking".

## 1. Organization

- **You borrow a framework and map it onto ML.** "Making Better Predictions" is built on ideas from Tetlock's *Superforecasting*: hedgehog vs. fox, "dragonfly eyes," "cooperation without deference." "Thinking About Thinking" is built on Bloom's taxonomy. In both, you treat humans and ML models as two versions of the same problem. Tetlock is never named, though, even though most of the section ideas are his.
- **Each section pairs a general explanation with your own take.** The Bloom post does this for every level. It explains the level in neutral terms, then follows with "I believe…", "True understanding, to me…", or "In my experience…". That second paragraph is where your actual voice and value come through.
- **You open broad and close neatly.** The openings make big claims ("Humans, much like machine learning models, are constantly making predictions" and the ML growth statistics). The closings are one-sentence wrap-ups.
- **Your heading levels are inconsistent.** In the predictions post, the intro has no heading. Then "Balancing Leadership and Forecasting," "Humility and Confidence," and "Auftragstaktik" sit as `###` under "Diversity Drives Better Predictions," but they aren't sub-points of diversity. The Bloom post uses only `###`.
- **The predictions post drifts.** It starts with personal forecasting and ends on military-style delegation (Auftragstaktik), agile vs. waterfall, and Amazon pivoting. The paragraph about the gap between offline and production model performance under "Track Your Predictions" is also tangential.
- **The Bloom post makes a promise it doesn't keep.** It asks which human insights can enhance large language models (LLMs) and says "I will explore these questions," but never comes back to them.

## 2. Voice and Habits

- **Measured, hedged tone.** You use "in my opinion," "I believe," and "I do not necessarily believe every team should…". You tend to present both sides and land on "it depends on team culture, talent distribution, and company priorities."
- **A recurring list of the full ML stack.** It shows up at least four times in slightly different forms:
  - "modeling, services, pipelines, infrastructure, or products"
  - "data, cloud native applications, modeling, infrastructure, and user understanding"
  - "data, model, app, or infrastructure stack"

  That's clearly how you see ML work, and it's a real point of view. It could be stated once as a thesis.
- **Analogies are your best tool.** Random forest as fox and gradient boosting as hedgehog is the most memorable idea in either post. Getting stuck in a local maximum vs. hitting a career plateau, and Steph Curry's confidence coming from preparation, also work well.
- **Engineer's shorthand:**
  - Slash compounds like "stakeholder/user feedbacks," "scala/java/python/framework development tax," and "training/deployment"
  - Jargon like "first order estimate," "flywheel," and "development tax"
  - "etc.." with a double period
- **Two registers.** Some paragraphs are polished but generic: "rapidly evolving knowledge landscape," "cohesive view," "robust," and "honing in" twice in one paragraph. They read like a textbook or LLM draft. The paragraphs about your own work are rougher but specific and credible. A reader can easily tell the two apart.
